Overview

The Quantum Nexus Lab is dedicated to advancing the frontiers of quantum information sciences. Our research spans the theoretical and algorithmic foundations of quantum technologies, with a particular emphasis on their applications to communication systems.

At the core of our work is a commitment to understanding the fundamental limits of quantum systems—from channel capacities and entanglement distributions to the performance bounds of quantum sensing and computing. We combine tools from quantum information theory, machine learning, and complexity theory to develop practical solutions for next-generation quantum networks.

Our work is characterized by a unique blend of theory-driven inquiry and application-focused innovation, bridging the gap between fundamental quantum science and its real-world deployment in communication systems.

Quantum Communications

Our research in quantum communications focuses on the fundamental performance limits of quantum channels and the development of protocols for secure and efficient information transmission. We investigate the channel capacity of various quantum communication channels, including discrete Weyl channels [2019, 2020], and explore the interplay between quantum technologies and non-terrestrial networks [2024].

A key contribution is the development of variational methods for estimating capacity bounds, enabling practical characterization of complex quantum channels [2022]. We also explore the simultaneous communication and parameter estimation of Pauli channels, pushing the boundaries of what can be achieved with limited quantum resources [2022].

The group has made significant advances in MIMO quantum communication in correlated pure-loss channels, establishing foundational results on diversity and multiplexing in quantum channels [2025, 2025]. Our work also addresses the practical aspects of quantum key distribution [2017] and quantum anonymous protocols [2021].

Quantum Computing

The group investigates quantum computing algorithms for practical problems in communication systems. We have developed quantum optimization algorithms for resource allocation problems, including the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) for knapsack resource allocation [2023] and for LEO satellite communications with cell-free massive MIMO [2023].

Our work on variational quantum computing includes error-mitigated photonic variational quantum eigensolvers [2022] and quantum property learning for NISQ networks [2025]. We also explore the robustness of quantum algorithms for blockchain consensus [2022] and optimal task scheduling in 6G networks [2024].

A significant contribution is our work on evolutionary algorithms and quantum computing, reviewing recent advances, opportunities, and challenges at the intersection of these fields [2025].

Quantum Sensing & Metrology

Our research in quantum sensing focuses on the fundamental limits of measurement precision and the development of practical protocols for parameter estimation. A landmark contribution is the demonstration of quantum-enhanced multiple-phase estimation using multi-mode NOON states, published in Nature Communications [2021].

We derive practical sensitivity bounds for multiple-phase estimation [2022] and establish the optimal strategy for such measurements [2022]. Our work also includes robust quantum state tomography methods for quantum sensing [2022] and entanglement-free parameter estimation of generalized Pauli channels [2021].

We also explore the intersection of sensing and security, developing quantum secure metrology for network-based applications [2023].

Quantum Networks & NISQ Systems

A major focus of the lab is on the engineering of quantum networks in the Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) era. Our comprehensive review on quantum network engineering [2024] outlines the principles, missions, and challenges of building practical quantum networks.

We have pioneered approaches to flexible entanglement routing using reinforcement learning, enabling the delivery of end-to-end entanglement while optimizing for fidelity and rate [2025]. Our work on traffic-aware minimum-cost quantum communication network planning [2025] provides a practical framework for optimizing quantum network resources.

We explore the integration of quantum technologies with non-terrestrial networks [2024] and quantum anonymous protocols for privacy in distributed networks [2022]. Our recent work on quantum network applications in 6G paradigms [2025] connects our research to next-generation communication systems.
