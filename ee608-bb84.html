BB84 Quantum Key Distribution

Information versus disturbance

Eve's probability of guessing a \(Z\)-basis key bit against Bob's error rate \(e\) in the \(X\) basis. The black dot marks the current settings.
Intercept and resend: \(P_{\text{guess}} = \tfrac12 + e\)
Controlled-rotation probe: \(P_{\text{guess}} = \tfrac12 + \sqrt{e(1-e)}\)

Secret key rate

\(r = 1 - 2h(Q)\) secret bits per sifted bit, with \(h\) the binary entropy. A key requires \(r > 0\), that is \(Q \lesssim 11\%\).

1. Encoding

Alice encodes a random bit \(b_i\) in a random basis \(\beta_i \in \{Z, X\}\). Bob measures in his own random basis \(\beta_i'\).

2. Eve and the channel

Eve

Eve measures each intercepted qubit in a random basis and sends Bob the state she found.

Eve's probe starts in \(\ket{0}_E\). The controlled rotation maps \(\ket{1}\ket{0}_E \to \ket{1}\left(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\ket{0}_E + \sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\ket{1}_E\right)\) and leaves \(\ket{0}\ket{0}_E\) unchanged. At \(\theta = 180^\circ\) it is the CNOT.

Exercises

  1. With no eavesdropper and no noise, send a few batches of qubits. What fraction of the rounds survives sifting, and what is \(Q\)?
  2. Let Eve intercept every qubit and run 100000 rounds. Check that \(Q = 25\%\) and that Eve's guesses agree with the sifted key in \(75\%\) of the rounds.
  3. Show that intercepting a fraction \(f\) of the qubits gives \(Q = f/4\). Above which \(f\) do Alice and Bob abort?
  4. Choose the dephasing channel and compare the error rates in the \(Z\) and \(X\) bases with the Lecture 8 result.
  5. Choose the depolarizing channel. Find the largest \(p\) for which \(r > 0\).
  6. Choose the controlled-rotation probe and set \(\theta\) so that \(e = 11\%\). Compare Eve's guessing probability with the Lecture 9 value \(0.81\), and with intercept and resend at the same \(e\).
